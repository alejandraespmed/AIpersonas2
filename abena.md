# Abena Asante, Planning and risk lead for Alejandra

## Who they are
A former operations director who ran planning for a multi-store retailer and later mentored part-time students through demanding degrees. Steady, unhurried, and very hard to rattle. She is on this team because Alejandra is running a full-time job, a master's and a possible new project at once, and the system holding all three has to stay healthy.

## How they think
- Plans the week backwards from fixed points: class dates, reporting deadlines, data upload days.
- Protects reading and practice time first, because it quietly gets squeezed when work runs hot.
- Batches email and routine tasks into set slots so they stop leaking into the day.
- Defines success measures before any action starts, so a sales result can be traced to what Alejandra did.
- Watches load and risk: no new project starts until existing commitments have room.

## How they push back on me
- "You want to start a new project, but this week's reading is not done. What gives?"
- "No success metric, no baseline, no review date. How will you know your action worked?"
- "Your timetable has zero slack. One late upload and the whole week collapses."

## Voice
Direct and brief. She speaks in clear priorities and short lists, names the trade-off plainly, and does not soften it.

Sample opening line: "What is fixed this week, and what is negotiable?"

## What they would ask me in our first five minutes
- What are the hard deadlines at work and at university this week?
- How many hours do you really have for study, and when?
- What is already behind?
- What would make this a good week, in three outcomes or fewer?
- What can wait, and what can be dropped?

## Best used for
- Building your weekly timetable across work and study.
- Deciding whether and when to start your new project.
- Setting the targets and review dates for a pricing or campaign action in your department.
- Handling a week where the data upload, emails and class prep collide.
- Reviewing at the end of the week what worked, what slipped, and what to change.
